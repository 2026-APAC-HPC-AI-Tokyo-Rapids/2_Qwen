Why: in kernel bench 39382077358 (image 39379223361, prefill-balanced-4096,
ranks 0/1/3 at the same SM clock as the control) the host-switched
2-math-warpgroup m64n256 tile shortened the K loops (ML1Loop 674-677 ->
619-631 us per CTA, ML2Loop 380-385 -> 331-344 us) but gave most of it back:
ML1Epi 63 -> 97-101 us, ML2Epi 25-26 -> 38 us, ML2Scatter 165-181 ->
186-204 us, ML1MmaWait 7 -> 68-74 us; MLastL2End about equal, kernel about
-2%. The FC2 part cannot be won back: ML2Busy is 579 us per CTA in both
variants and the rank's FC2 phase (MLastL1End -> MLastL2End) is 590-600 us in
both, so FC2 is bound by the NVLink combine traffic and a shorter FC2 loop or
epilogue only moves time into the scatter. This commit therefore targets the
compute-bound FC1 phase (about 780 us of the 1520 us kernel):

- FC1 epilogue. The SASS of the current 2-warpgroup build shows the SwiGLU as
  32 small branch regions per thread (BSSY/BRA from the per-pair valid_r0 /
  valid_r1 checks), each a serial FMUL -> EX2 -> FADD -> RCP -> FMUL -> FMUL
  chain of two elements plus the non-ftz EX2 range fix-up. With 2 math warps
  per scheduler instead of 4 the MUFU latency is exposed (2.5 -> 3.9 us per
  tile). The staging stores are 2-byte STS into 64 rows of 128 bytes with no
  pad, so the 8 rows of a WGMMA fragment hit the same 2 banks (8-way, 2048
  wavefronts per tile, twice the 4-warpgroup layout), followed by a
  warpgroup barrier, a TMA store and a full store drain before the arrival
  (ML1Store about 1.2 us per tile).
- FC1 convoy. Both warpgroups wait on the same stage, issue at the same time
  and their groups finish together, so per K block the tensor pipe idles
  while both rescale 128 accumulator registers (2 warps x 128 FMUL per
  scheduler, about 256 cycles). 1280 cycles per K block measured against 1024
  of MMA work matches that.

What: patches/deep-gemm-bench/fc1-2wg-host-epi-turn/ = 01 sf128 + 02 swizzle
(copies of the overlay patches) + 03-sm90-prefill-2wg-host-epi-turn.patch,

- FC1 epilogue for 2 math warpgroups: SwiGLU for every element without row
  branches (the row masks only gate the amax and the stores), EX2 in the
  .ftz form (e below 2^-126 only occurs where 1 + e rounds to 1 either way),
  amax with FMNMX on absolute values. Same SwiGLU, per-row amax over the
  warpgroup's 128 output columns (one per-128 group), E4M3 scale and
  saturating conversion as before, so the FC1 output bytes and scales are
  identical. Each warp stages its own 16 rows in a 16 x 144-byte slice (the
  16-byte pad puts the 8 fragment rows on distinct banks, conflict-free) and
  writes them to the L1-output pool with 16-byte global stores, 8 lanes per
  128-byte row segment, valid rows only. No warpgroup barrier, no TMA store,
  no drain.
- FC1 readiness per warp: kL1OutputArrivalParts = 4 for this geometry, so the
  FC2 A loader expects 12 n blocks x active warpgroups x 4 arrivals. Each
  warp publishes its arrival (red.release.gpu by lane 0 after a warp sync)
  late: in the next FC1 tile's K block 2, by when the stores have long
  completed, or, for the CTA's last FC1 tile, in the math warps' FC2 claim
  wrapper before the first take_l2_block. Every CTA calls that wrapper at
  least once after its FC1 loop, also when it gets no FC2 block, so no
  arrival can be left unpublished (the deadlock noted for the earlier
  store-defer design). The A loader issues fence.proxy.async.global after
  its acquire spin, ordering the generic stores before its TMA loads.
- FC1 WGMMA issue alternates between the warpgroups with named barriers 8
  (WG0's turn) and 9 (WG1's turn): WG0 issues the group of a K block after
  WG1 issued its previous one (also across tiles; not before WG0's very first
  FC1 group), WG1 after WG0 issued the same K block, each arriving on the
  partner's barrier right after its issue. The turn is passed at issue, not
  at completion (the 4-warpgroup mma-order of 39213738748 passed it after
  wait<2>), as in FlashAttention-3's warp scheduler barrier, so the tensor
  pipe holds the partner's group while one warpgroup waits, releases the
  stage and rescales. Counts: barrier 9 gets N arrivals from WG0 and N syncs
  from WG1, barrier 8 N arrivals from WG1 and N - 1 syncs from WG0 plus one in
  the FC2 claim wrapper (only if WG0 issued). Each arrival on a barrier is
  ordered after the previous phase's sync by the issue chain, so no phase
  gets two arrivals from one warpgroup; waits only point to earlier issues
  and epilogues have no cross-warpgroup barrier, so there is no cycle. Both
  warpgroups run every mainloop (rows past valid_m only skip the epilogue),
  so N is equal. The FC2 loop is unchanged.
